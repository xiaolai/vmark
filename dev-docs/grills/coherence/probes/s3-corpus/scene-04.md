# Scene 4 — Cullet

The market smelled of tar, bream, and other people's breakfasts. Elena
worked her way down Saltmarket Row with an empty sack and a full purse,
and meant to reverse the two before noon.

The cullet-seller wanted three crowns for the crate of broken glass.
She looked at the crate, looked at him, and offered one.

"It's good colour," he said. "Bottle-green. Your father knows what
that's worth."

"My father knows what sand is worth," she said. "That's what he makes
it from. This is his off-cuts sold back to him at a year's interest."

He came down to two crowns. She turned one coin over in her fingers,
slow as tide, and said nothing at all. The pause did her haggling for
her. It usually did.

Across the quay she caught sight of old Marcus arguing with a
fish-seller over something, and she lifted a hand to him. He lifted one
back without breaking stride in the argument.

The cullet-seller cracked at one crown and four pennies. Elena counted
them out, hoisted the crate, and let herself smile at the harbour on
the way back — a small dry smile, cost included, no charge for the
seller's education.

Her father would grumble at the green. He grumbled at every colour that
was not the one he needed. She patted the compass in her coat pocket
and took the long way home, well clear of the deep-water stairs.
