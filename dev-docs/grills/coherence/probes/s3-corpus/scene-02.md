# Scene 2 — Trimming the light

The kelp tea had gone bitter enough to strip paint, which was how Marcus
liked it. He took the lighthouse stairs one at a time, pot in hand,
letting the right leg have its say on every step. Fifty-odd years and a
war in that leg. It had earned the right to complain.

Pitch was waiting on the gallery rail, feathers puffed against the wind.

"You've eaten," Marcus said. "I can tell. You've the look of a raven
that's eaten."

The bird said nothing, which was its way of agreeing.

He set the pot down and got to work on the wick, hands steady from long
habit. Below, the sea worried at the rocks the way it had every night
since he took the post. Some keepers drank. He had seen what that did on
the wall of a different tower, years back, and he poured himself more
tea instead, black and bitter as tar.

The lamp caught. Light swung out over the water, found nothing, kept
looking.

"Right then," he said to Pitch. "Another night of nobody drowning."

The raven sidled along the rail and pulled at his sleeve, and the old
soldier let it, the way he let no one else.
