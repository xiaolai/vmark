# Scene 6 — The gallery ledger

Wind from the north-east, glass falling. Marcus wrote it in the ledger
the way he wrote it every dusk, and Pitch stood on the ledger's corner
holding down the page as if the tower would blow away without him.

Two years he had kept the light now. It felt like ten and also like a
fortnight. The leg ached on the gallery stairs the way it always did in
a north-easter, and he gave it its due groan and got on with the round.

Four springs since the fog lifted — he still measured the years by
that, everyone on this coast did. Two ruined harvests, then that first
clean spring, and then the summer the fleet burned before anyone had
finished being grateful. He had watched that glow from a troop ship,
too far out to be any use, and it had made up his mind about the sea:
better to keep a light on it than sail it.

And the bay itself — the drowned quarter was older than all of that.
The year before he first shipped out, the sea had taken it in a night.
Twenty-two years gone now. He had marched past the ruin of it as a
young man and thought it someone else's bad luck. The sea had been
patient with him ever since, the way a creditor is patient.

He capped the ink. Pitch stepped off the page.

"Don't gloat," Marcus told him. "You held down one page. I climbed the
stairs."
