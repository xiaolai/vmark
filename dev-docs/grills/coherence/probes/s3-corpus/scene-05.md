# Scene 5 — The grey sash

She felt the working die in her hands before she understood why, and
then she saw the sash and understood everything.

The inspector was younger than his voice. Grey silk across the chest,
iron rod loose in one hand, the bored walk of a man who had done this
a hundred times inside these walls and been right ninety times.

"Unlicensed working," he said. "Within the city. You know what the
charter says."

Elena knew. Everyone on Saltmarket Row knew. They took you to the
Guild-house and they heated the stamp and they set the wave-mark on the
inside of your left wrist, where every shopkeeper and every ferryman
could see it for the rest of your life. Second time, exile.

"I was warming my hands," she said.

"You were warming the tide." He tapped the rod against his boot. "Forty
crowns a year buys the right to do that legally. You have forty
crowns?"

"If I had forty crowns," she said, "I would buy a boat and be out of
your city."

It was the wrong month for a Guild-house full of witnesses — some
festival of theirs, wagons in the yard, clerks everywhere. She watched
him weigh the paperwork against the afternoon. The pause stretched.

"Off the Row," he said finally. "If I smell salt on you again, the
stamp will be hot before you finish lying to me."

She went. Both hands in her pockets, left wrist bare, and meant to keep
it that way.
