# Scene 1 — The drowned quarter

Salt and wet stone. Elena stood at the memorial rail where the old
harbour quarter had gone under, twenty-two years past, and let the wind
pull at her braid. The water below was green and deep and she kept both
boots planted well back from the edge. She had never learned to swim.
She never would.

Her father had sent her with a wreath of glass flowers, blown in his own
workshop on Saltmarket Row, each petal thin as a bubble. She held it in
her left hand, the good hand, and worked her mother's brass compass out
of her coat pocket with the other. The lid was worn smooth. Her thumb
knew the shape of it better than her eyes did.

Her mother had been a child when the sea took the quarter in a single
night. Houses, boat-sheds, the old chandlery — gone before the bells
could finish ringing. Every year the town came here, and every year
fewer of them remembered it themselves.

Elena looked at the green water until her grey eyes stung, then set the
wreath on the rail. She did not say anything. Words were her father's
trade even less than hers. She clicked the compass open, watched the
needle settle, and clicked it shut.

Twenty-two years. The sea kept what it took.
