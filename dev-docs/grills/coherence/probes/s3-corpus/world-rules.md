# The rules of tidebinding

1. Tidebinding works only within sight of the sea. Out of sight of open
   water a binding simply fails — no flare, no smell, nothing at all.
2. Every working is paid for in sleep: hours of dreamless sleep in
   proportion to the working's size, collected the following night whether
   the binder wills it or not.
3. Cold iron blocks a binding. Iron touching the binder's skin, or placed
   between binder and target, stops the working outright.
4. A completed working leaves a distinct smell of ozone and hot salt,
   noticeable to anyone nearby for a few minutes.
5. The talent is inborn; roughly one person in four hundred can bind.
