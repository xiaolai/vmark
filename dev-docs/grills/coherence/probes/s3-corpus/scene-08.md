# Scene 8 — The lens

The rowing was the worst part. Elena kept her grey eyes on Gullpoint
and not on the water under the hull, which was deep and green and none
of her business. Her hands did the oars. The rest of her recited the
number of boat-lengths to the lighthouse jetty and subtracted one at a
time.

Pitch met her at the top of the steps, sidling and croaking, and
escorted her up like a small black harbour-master.

"It's cracked, then," Marcus said, by way of hello.

"It's mended." She unwrapped the lamp lens from its straw. Her father
had ground the replacement ring himself and she had carried it across
open water, which he would never know the cost of. "Hold it level."

He held it level. She set her hands on the frame and did the small
quiet thing, and the ring settled into the old brass seat as if it had
grown there. The air snapped faintly. Ozone and hot salt drifted
through the lamp room, and Marcus wrinkled his nose at it like a man
smelling a lie.

"Parlour trick," he said, without heat. He was no binder and wanted no
part of it, but a keeper who turned away a true-set lens was a fool,
and he was not a fool.

He made tea while the wind came up. The Guild's name came up too, the
way it always did with him. He had no love for the Guild and said so,
twice, and Elena drank her tea and let the second time pass without
comment.

The row back was easier. Rowing away from deep water always was.
