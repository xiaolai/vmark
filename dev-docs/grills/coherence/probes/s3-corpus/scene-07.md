# Scene 7 — The debt

The smell would not wash out. Ozone and hot salt, faint but stubborn,
clinging to her coat like woodsmoke after a bonfire. The baker's boy
had wrinkled his nose at her as she passed; she supposed the working
still hung about her clothes.

It had been too big a working, that was the truth of it. The cistern
crack had run a full arm-span before her father noticed the puddle, and
she had drawn it closed in one go instead of three small evenings like
a sensible binder. Now the evening was here and the price was calling
in its markers.

Elena sat on the workshop step with her braid coming loose and fought
to keep her eyes open. It was not tiredness, not exactly. It was the
tide going out with her behind it. Tonight there would be no dreams,
only the long dark ledger squaring itself, and tomorrow she would wake
grey and slow and owing nothing.

Down the Row, Lantern Guild banners flapped above the counting-house —
some festival of theirs. The bunting snapped in the wind like little
sails. She was too tired to care what they were celebrating.

Her father brought her bread and did not ask why she was falling asleep
at six in the evening. He had stopped asking questions whose answers he
did not want. She turned her mother's compass over in her pocket, once,
twice, and let her eyes close before the sun was properly down.

The sleep took her like deep water. It was the only deep water she had
never feared.
