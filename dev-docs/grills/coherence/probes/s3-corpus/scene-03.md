# Scene 3 — The hollow

The boy went inland to be safe, which showed how little he knew.

In a hollow between two sheep fields, out of sight of any water, he
unwrapped the stolen charm and tried the trick again. Nothing. No flare,
no smell, not so much as a warm feeling in his fingers. He might as well
have been whistling at the grass. He shook the charm, tried again,
got nothing again, and sat down hard in the bracken to think.

It had worked on the cliff path. It had worked once in the alley behind
the fish market too, before it suddenly hadn't — though that time he'd
had the iron nail in the same pocket, the one he carried against luck,
and the trick had died the moment his knuckles brushed it. He dug the
nail out now and threw it into the gorse.

Then he climbed out of the hollow, and from the top of the rise he could
see it: a grey line of sea between two hills, far off but plain.

He tried the trick a third time. The air snapped. Ozone and hot salt,
sharp as a slap, and the charm sang in his hand the way it had on the
cliff path.

He whooped once, then remembered, and went quiet. Tonight it would want
its sleep from him. Last time it had taken the whole night and left him
grey until noon. He looked at the far-off water and wondered, too late,
what a big trick would cost.
