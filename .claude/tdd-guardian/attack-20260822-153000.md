## The nine gaps

**Gap 1 — `overflowState`: epsilon required at one endpoint, free at the other.**
An impl with `left > EPS` but `left < overflow` (no epsilon right) passes every
case: law 2 (completeness) is satisfied *by the lying flag itself*. Real WebKit
produces fractional `scrollWidth` constantly. Missing: `{scrollLeft:200,
clientWidth:400, scrollWidth:600.4}` ⇒ `{canScrollLeft:true, canScrollRight:false}`
(S3), and restate law 1 two-sided: `canScrollRight === (scrollWidth - clientWidth
- scrollLeft > ε)`. The generator already reaches the counterexample; no law judges it.

**Gap 2 — `useTabStripOverflow`: a container attached AFTER mount is never observed.**
`useEffect(..., [])` with an early `if (!node) return` is permanent for the session.
`StatusBarTabStrip.tsx:90` renders the tablist as `{showTabs && <div…>}` and the
component stays mounted, so `ref.current` goes `null → node` with no effect re-run
and the chevrons never appear again. Missing: the ref transition both directions
(S2) — assert the ResizeObserver fake's constructor ran exactly once *after*
attachment. Also: state the fixture's NESTING (shipped tree is scroll region >
tablist > tab > span > text); a flat fixture makes `subtree` a free parameter.

**Gap 3 — `tabMruStore`: the generated law covers activations only; every removal
case asserts membership, never order.** A "prune by re-deriving from the live tab
list" impl passes everything and silently replaces the user's MRU with strip
order. Missing: extend the S6 reference-model alphabet to include
`close`/`removeWindow`, asserting after EVERY command. S1 anchor: activate A,B,C;
close B; `expect(mru(W)).toEqual([cId, aId])` — exact, not membership.

**Gap 4 — `tabMruStore`: the no-op contract is pinned on one of four ignore paths.**
Only the front-re-activation row counts subscriber calls; the other three assert
list contents, so a fresh outer object is invisible and a hot-exit restore of N
tabs fires N store notifications. Missing: S4 exhaustive over
`{front re-activation} ∪ (ActivationOrigin \ {"user"}) ∪ {null key}`, each with
`expect(spy).not.toHaveBeenCalled()`.

**Gap 5 — `tab.lastUsed`: the browser entry is only ever tested with ONE page.**
`pages[0]`, `pages.at(-1)` and `lastActiveBrowserPageId` are indistinguishable at
one page. With two open — the normal state — `Ctrl-Tab` lands on the wrong page and
looks like it worked. Missing (S2, two rows): P1,P2 with P2 active ⇒ two presses
return P2 (kills `pages[0]`); P1,P2 with P1 active ⇒ returns P1 (kills `at(-1)`).

**Gap 6 — `tab.lastUsed`: a DEAD KEY satisfies the stale-entry case; the closure
law is one-sided.** `setActiveTabGuarded` refuses a dead id, `activeTabId` stays
put, and "is live and is not B" is satisfied by the pre-existing active tab doing
nothing. Missing: make law 2 two-sided — closure AND progress.
`if (visibleWindowTabs(W).length >= 2) expect(after).not.toBe(before)` (S5). This
one clause kills the whole dead-key class, including Gap 5's stale-id failure.

**Gap 7 — `buildQuickOpenItems`: the "empty MRU" case MANDATES the defect, in the
state that occurs on every launch.** D4 says the MRU is not persisted, so it is
empty at every cold start. A `if (mru.length === 0) return legacyBuild(...)`
branch satisfies "an empty MRU leaves the previous order intact" *by
construction* — preserving recents-win dedup, which is the work item's entire
subject. Missing (S2): same input at both MRU populations; recents `["/r.md"]`
not open, open `/a.md`,`/b.md`. MRU `[]` ⇒ `["/a.md","/b.md","/r.md"]`; MRU
`["/b.md","/a.md"]` ⇒ `["/b.md","/a.md","/r.md"]`. `/r.md` last in BOTH.
Also: the two laws are stated over two different functions — conservation belongs
to `buildQuickOpenItems`, but `filterAndRankItems` drops the whole `workspace`
tier on an empty query (`useQuickOpenItems.ts:124`). Say which law owns which.

**Gap 8 — `handleTabClosed`: THE ENTIRE UNIT IS GREEN AGAINST THE SHIPPED CODE IT
EXISTS TO REPLACE.** The do-nothing implementation is the current
`paneStore.ts:179-198`, verbatim. Under R2 the only new observable is
`activeTabId` after a paned close, and nothing asserts it; the property's clause
(c) short-circuits at `if (!split?.enabled) return`. The three discriminating
cases still read "decided by G1". Missing: assert `activeTabId` after a paned
close **with strip order `[A, C, B]`** — with the obvious `[A,B,C]` fixture the
neighbour pick (`tabStoreHelpers.ts:184-193`, `remaining[min(removedIndex,
len-1)]`) *coincidentally equals the survivor* and the do-nothing impl passes.
Close A ⇒ `primaryTabId: bId`, `activeTabId === bId`, `.not.toBe(cId)`; close B ⇒
`aId`, `.not.toBe(cId)`. Add property clause (e): if the closed tab was in a pane
of an enabled split, `activeTabId` is the OTHER pane's tab, never a third tab.

**Gap 9 — pane indicator: R3's suppression is asserted nowhere, and the
selector's signature cannot express it.** The S4 domain is the nine `WindowSplit`
values; `browserWorkspaceActive` is not a dimension of it, so R3 is unstateable
there. A screen-reader user gets an `aria-description` about a pane that is not
rendered. Missing (S2): identical split object, `browserWorkspaceActive` true vs
false ⇒ `[data-pane-indicator]` length 0 vs 1, and no `aria-description` in the
browser-active render. **And record the placement** — widen the selector domain to
18 rows, or state that R3 lives in the component. Left unsaid, it lands in neither.
